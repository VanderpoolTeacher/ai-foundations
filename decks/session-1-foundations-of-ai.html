Start here · my three rules

AI drafts it. I send it.

  • AI drafts, a person sends.Sending is a decision. Decisions stay with people.
  • If it doesn’t know, it guesses.It never tells you what it wasn’t sure about. A screenshot saying “Tuesday at 3” has no year in it — it will pick a Tuesday and sound certain. Make it ask you instead.
  • Verify before you trust it.“No error” is not the same as “it worked.” Read the result back.
Everything else in this hour is an explanation of why these three rules exist.
What goes wrong

Bias, data privacy, and security

Bias

It repeats the past

It learned from what already happened. If past decisions favoured one kind of person, it will too — not because it is broken, but because it is working correctly on the wrong data.

Privacy

The prompt box is a public website

Anything pasted into a public tool has left the building. Apprentice names, SSNs, salary data, employer contracts.

Security

Wrong in ways that look right

A made-up citation. A policy that doesn't exist. A number that's close. If nobody checks, it becomes the record.

Machine learning

Show it enough examples and it finds the pattern

How it works
Show it thousands of examples that are already labelled. It works out what they have in common, then applies that to something new.
For example
Ten thousand emails marked spam or not-spam. It learns what spam looks like, then sorts tomorrow's mail.
What you get back
A label, a score, or a number. Never new content.
What it cannot do
Tell you why it decided that. Handle anything unlike what it has already seen.

Generative AI

It predicts the most likely next word

How it works
It read an enormous amount of text and learned which words tend to follow which. Given your prompt, it produces the most likely next word — one at a time.
For example
You ask for a job posting. It writes the most likely job posting, word by word.
What you get back
New content — text, images, code. A first draft.
What it cannot do
Know what is true. It only knows what is likely — which is why it guesses instead of asking.

How normal software works

A recipe gives the same result every time. A program can make decisions.

A recipe

Fixed steps

Same ingredients in, same cake out. Every time. No judgement anywhere in it.

A program

Steps plus decisions

If the invoice is over $5,000, route it to Jody. Still predictable — you can read it and know exactly what it will do.

Run either one twice and you get the same answer twice.
Why AI is different

Run the same prompt twice and you can get two different answers

A recipe and a program both give you the same answer every time. That is what makes them checkable — you can read them and know what they will do.

AI is the first tool on your desk that does not work that way.

No other software on your computer behaves like this. It is the single reason all three rules exist.

Where this is going

That’s a system, not a prompt

Step one

Messy input

A photo, a screenshot, a transcript, forty survey responses. Something a person would have had to read and retype.

Step two

Drafted output

Structured, usable, and roughly right. Never final.

Step three

A person checks

The step people skip. It is the step that makes the whole thing safe to use.

A prompt gets you one answer. A system does the same job every time, with the same guardrails. Both of those demos run this way whether I am paying attention or not — that is the difference between using AI and building with it.
